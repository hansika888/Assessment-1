🍽️ The Balanced Plate

A simple way to build a nutritious main meal.

🥬 Vegetables & salad

Make vegetables a major part of the meal. Use a variety of colours and seasonal choices.

🌾 Grains / starch

Choose roti, rice, daliya, oats or another staple according to your needs.

🥣 Protein

Include dal, beans, chana, curd, paneer, eggs, fish, meat or soy foods as appropriate.

🍊 Fruit & healthy fats

Add fruit across the day and small amounts of nuts, seeds or healthy cooking oils.

🩺 Nutrition & Health Conditions

General food principles — not individual medical prescriptions.

Diabetes / Prediabetes

Emphasize vegetables, pulses, whole grains and other high-fibre foods. Limit sugary drinks and frequent refined snack foods. Pair carbohydrate foods with protein or other nutrient-dense foods.

High Blood Pressure

Emphasize vegetables, fruits, whole grains, pulses and appropriate dairy. Reduce excess sodium from salty packaged foods, pickles, papad and heavily salted meals.

Iron-Deficiency Anemia

Include iron-rich foods such as pulses, legumes, leafy greens and suitable animal foods. Vitamin-C-rich foods can help improve absorption of non-heme iron from plant foods.

High Cholesterol

Increase fibre-rich foods such as oats, barley, pulses, vegetables and fruits. Prefer unsaturated fats and limit trans fats and excess saturated fat.

Kidney Disease

Kidney diets are highly individualized. Protein, sodium, potassium, phosphorus and fluid may need adjustment according to kidney function and medical advice.

Celiac Disease

Gluten must be avoided strictly. Naturally gluten-free foods include rice, maize, potatoes, pulses, fruits, vegetables and plain dairy. Cross-contact with gluten-containing foods must also be considered.

⚠️ Important: This website is for nutrition education. A person with a diagnosed disease, pregnancy, severe anemia, kidney disease, diabetes requiring medication, an eating disorder or another medical condition should receive an individualized plan from a qualified doctor and/or registered dietitian.

🌼 Healthy Habits

💧 Hydration

Drink water regularly and pay attention to thirst, weather and activity.

🌈 Variety

Rotate different vegetables, fruits, grains and protein foods across the week.

🥫 Less ultra-processed food

Keep packaged snacks, sugary drinks and heavily processed foods occasional rather than the foundation of meals.

⚖️ Balance

A healthy diet is about the overall pattern, not one “perfect” food.

How the calculator works

For adults, estimated resting energy expenditure is calculated using the Mifflin–St Jeor equation and then adjusted using an activity factor.

BMI is calculated from weight and height as a screening measure. It is not a diagnosis and should not be interpreted using adult cut-offs for children and teenagers.

People under 18 need age- and sex-specific growth assessment rather than an adult calorie prescription.
